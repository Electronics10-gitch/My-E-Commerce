# Statement of the Problem

## Context
Online electronics retail in Nigeria is fragmented across marketplaces
(Konga, Jumia, AliExpress, Slot, Jiji and others), each with its own
pricing, seller verification, and delivery process. UTAUT2 identifies
hedonic motivation, price value, and habit as the added drivers of
consumer technology adoption beyond the original acceptance model — all
three are weakened when a buyer cannot easily compare price value across
sellers, or has no habitual, trusted place to shop.

Trust is the central psychological currency of e-commerce: a transaction
happens under information asymmetry (the buyer cannot inspect the item),
spatial separation (buyer and seller are not in the same place), and
temporal separation (payment happens before delivery). A buyer must trust
the seller before the exchange completes, and in a fragmented market that
trust has to be rebuilt from scratch on every platform.

## The problem
A buyer shopping for electronics in this market cannot, on one screen:

1. See a product's price next to what comparable marketplaces charge, to
   judge price value before committing.
2. Buy confidently from an independent seller with the same trust
   guarantees as buying from the platform itself.
3. Track an order through delivery, and raise a return without leaving
   the platform or waiting on unclear support channels.
4. Confirm the platform is handling their personal data and consumer
   rights in line with recognised law and standards (NDPA 2023, GDPR
   where applicable, SON/SONCAP, ISO quality and security references).

## Who this affects
- **Buyers**, who bear the information asymmetry and the risk of paying
  before receiving goods.
- **Independent sellers**, who need a storefront and trust signals they
  cannot build alone.
- **The platform operator**, who is legally and reputationally
  accountable for data handling and consumer protection regardless of
  who fulfils an order.

## Proposed direction
Build a single marketplace — ElectroMart — where buyers and sellers
share one catalogue, every product carries a live-style price
comparison against other marketplaces, every order is trackable and
returnable from within the app, and the platform's data-protection and
standards posture is documented and visible, not assumed. This is
developed as the course's e-commerce deliverable, following the
Analysis → Design → Development → Testing → Deployment lifecycle.
